# The Falling Jolt Before Sleep (Bean Stories short #12) - target <= 41 s

## Facts (sourced)
- Hypnic jerk (sleep start / hypnagogic jerk): an involuntary myoclonic twitch at sleep onset, often with a falling
  sensation. Prevalence 60-70% of people (PubMed 28007358; TIME "sleep start"; Wikipedia: ~70% at least once,
  ~10% daily).
- Proposed explanation: as muscles relax at sleep onset, the brain misinterprets the relaxation as falling and
  triggers a jolt (common explanation; cause not established).
- Hypothesis: an archaic reflex - the brain misreading sleep-onset relaxation as a sleeping primate falling out of a
  tree (Wikipedia "Hypnic jerk"; often attributed to Coolidge). Presented as "some scientists think".

## Voice-over (Liam)
That jolt right before you fall asleep? Your brain thinks you're falling... out of a tree.
It's called a hypnic jerk, and up to seventy percent of people get them.
Here's one idea why.
As you drift off, your muscles go completely limp.
But your brain is still half awake. It feels your body suddenly drop... and panics.
We're falling!
So it fires every muscle at once.
And some scientists think it's an ancient reflex.
Millions of years ago, our ancestors slept up in trees.
Relax too much... and you'd fall.
A quick jolt woke them up before they hit the ground.
So next time it happens, your brain just saved you... from a tree you're not even in.
