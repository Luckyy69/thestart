Astronauts can't burp. Not won't. Can't. On Earth, gravity does a quiet job for you. The gas floats up, the food stays down. Burp. In space, nothing floats up. Gas and food mix into one bubbly soup. So when an astronaut burps, it's not just air that comes out. Astronauts call it a wet burp. Now guess who still wanted to send fizzy drinks to space. In 1985, Coca-Cola and Pepsi flew special cans on the space shuttle Challenger. The crew opened them. Took a big sip. And the bubbles had nowhere to go. Wet. Burps. Neither soda made it onto the menu. But astronaut Jim Newman found a trick. He pushed off a wall. Hard. For a split second, his body felt something like gravity. Gas up. Food down. One perfect burp. Billions of dollars in rocket science... and you still need a wall to burp.
