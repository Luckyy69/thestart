# "The Skyscraper That Cooked a Car" — original script v1 (2026-11-22)
Facts (sources):
- 20 Fenchurch Street, London ("Walkie-Talkie"), architect Rafael Viñoly. Concave south face acted as a concave mirror,
  focusing sunlight on the street to the south for up to ~2 h/day in direct sun. Nicknamed "Walkie-Scorchie"/"Fryscraper". (Wikipedia, 20 Fenchurch Street)
- Late Aug / early Sept 2013: a Jaguar XJ parked on Eastcheap had its wing mirror, badge and panels melted/warped;
  developers paid the owner £946 for repairs. (Channel 4 News 3 Sep 2013; ITV News 3 Sep 2013; Wikipedia)
- A City A.M. reporter fried an egg in a pan in the beam. (City A.M., "Eggsclusive")
- Crowds came to see it. (Bloomberg, 5 Sep 2013)
- Fix: permanent brise soleil of horizontal aluminium fins on the south face, levels 3-34, planning OK May 2014. (Construction Index; ENR)
- Viñoly's Vdara hotel, Las Vegas, 2010: south facade focused sun on the pool deck, a guest's plastic newspaper bag melted;
  staff nicknamed it the "death ray". (NBC News; FindLaw; Architectural Record)

## Voice-over (English, one steady tone)
A London skyscraper melted a car, just by standing in the sun.
It's 2013, and the city has a shiny new tower, curved like a giant walkie-talkie.
One sunny day, a businessman parks his Jaguar on the street below and goes to work.
When he comes back, the mirror has drooped, the badge has gone soft, and the panels are warped like candle wax.
Word gets around. A reporter walks into the beam with a frying pan, cracks an egg, and fries it right there on the pavement.
Soon crowds are showing up just to stand in the hot spot.
Luckily, it only happened for about two hours a day.
Unluckily, it happened every sunny day.
You see, a flat window bounces sunlight straight back.
But a curved wall of glass bends all those reflections toward the same spot.
Ever burned a leaf with a magnifying glass? Same idea, except this magnifying glass was a skyscraper.
So the tower got a row of metal fins across its face, to break up the sunlight.
But here's the thing. The same architect had done it before.
Three years earlier, his hotel in Las Vegas melted a guest's bag by the pool.
The staff had a name for it: the death ray.
