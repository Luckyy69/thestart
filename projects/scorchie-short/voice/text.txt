A London skyscraper melted a car, just by standing in the sun. It's 2013, and the city has a shiny new tower, curved like a giant walkie-talkie. One sunny day, a businessman parks his Jaguar on the street below and goes to work. When he comes back, the mirror has drooped, the badge has gone soft, and the panels are warped like candle wax. Word gets around. A reporter walks into the beam with a frying pan, cracks an egg, and fries it right there on the pavement. Soon crowds are showing up just to stand in the hot spot. Luckily, it only happened for about two hours a day. Unluckily, it happened every sunny day. You see, a flat window bounces sunlight straight back. But a curved wall of glass bends all those reflections toward the same spot. Ever burned a leaf with a magnifying glass? Same idea, except this magnifying glass was a skyscraper. So the tower got a row of metal fins across its face, to break up the sunlight. But here's the thing. The same architect had done it before. Three years earlier, his hotel in Las Vegas melted a guest's bag by the pool. The staff had a name for it: the death ray.
