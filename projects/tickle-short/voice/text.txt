Try to tickle yourself. Go on, I'll wait. Nothing, right? That's not you. That's your brain. Every time you move, a part of your brain called the cerebellum predicts exactly what you're about to feel. And then it turns that feeling down. So when your own fingers come for your ribs, your brain already knows. No surprise. No tickle. But scientists in London wanted to beat the brain. So they built a tickle robot. You move a handle with one hand. The robot tickles your other hand. At first, it barely tickled. Then they added a tiny delay. Just a fraction of a second. And suddenly... it tickled. The longer the delay, the more it tickled. Your brain just couldn't keep up. So if you ever want to tickle yourself... you'll need a robot. A slightly slow one.
