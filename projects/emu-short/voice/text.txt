In 1932, Australia declared war on birds. And lost. After World War One, thousands of soldiers became wheat farmers in Western Australia. Then twenty thousand emus showed up and ate everything. So the farmers called the army. The army sent one major, two soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets. Easy, right? First ambush: a thousand emus at a watering hole. The gun jams after twelve. So they bolt a machine gun onto a truck. The emus run fifty kilometers an hour. The truck can't keep up. Then the birds split into small groups, each with its own lookout. Basically, guerrilla warfare. After weeks of fighting, the army goes home. The major said, if he had soldiers who could take bullets like these birds, he could beat any army in the world. It's still called the Great Emu War. Final score: Emus, one. Army, zero.
