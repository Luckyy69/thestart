# "The Rocket That Made Its Own Lightning" (Apollo 12) — original script v1 (2026-11-23)
Facts (sources):
- Apollo 12 launched 14 Nov 1969 from Kennedy Space Center in rain and overcast; crew Pete Conrad, Dick Gordon, Alan Bean. (NASA Apollo Flight Journal "Lightning strike")
- Struck by lightning at T+36.5 s and again at T+52 s. The vehicle and its long exhaust plume triggered the discharges; no natural lightning had been seen in the area. (NASA AFJ; NASA Science "Lightning strikes twice"; Live Science)
- Fuel cells dropped off line, warning lights lit, the guidance platform tumbled ("we just lost the platform"); Mission Control telemetry turned to garbage. (NASA SP-350 ch.7-3; AFJ)
- EECOM John Aaron recognised the pattern from a test about a year earlier and called "Try SCE to AUX"; Alan Bean knew the switch and flipped it; telemetry returned. (NASA AFJ; Universe Today)
- Intrepid landed on the Moon on 19 Nov 1969, five days after launch. (NASA)

## Voice-over (English, calm, story-first)
In 1969, a rocket on its way to the Moon got struck by lightning. Twice. And the rocket made that lightning itself.
It's a grey, rainy morning in Florida. Three astronauts sit on top of the biggest rocket ever built.
Liftoff. Thirty-six seconds later, a flash.
Every warning light in the cabin comes on at once.
Sixteen seconds later, a second bolt.
In Mission Control, the screens fill with garbage. Everyone is thinking the same word: abort.
Then a young engineer, John Aaron, says something nobody understands. "Try SCE to AUX."
A year earlier, he'd seen this exact mess of numbers in a test.
Up in the capsule, Alan Bean finds the switch and flips it. The screens come back to life.
But nobody saw any lightning that morning.
The rocket's fiery exhaust was so long, it acted like a giant wire between the clouds and the ground.
The rocket was its own lightning rod.
Five days later, they landed on the Moon.
