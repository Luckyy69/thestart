In 1969, a rocket on its way to the Moon got struck by lightning. Twice. And the rocket made that lightning itself. It's a grey, rainy morning in Florida. Three astronauts sit on top of the biggest rocket ever built. Liftoff. Thirty-six seconds later, a flash. Every warning light in the cabin comes on at once. Sixteen seconds later, a second bolt. In Mission Control, the screens fill with garbage. Everyone is thinking the same word: abort. Then a young engineer, John Aaron, says something nobody understands. "Try SCE to AUX." A year earlier, he'd seen this exact mess of numbers in a test. Up in the capsule, Alan Bean finds the switch and flips it. The screens come back to life. But nobody saw any lightning that morning. The rocket's fiery exhaust was so long, it acted like a giant wire between the clouds and the ground. The rocket was its own lightning rod. Five days later, they landed on the Moon.
