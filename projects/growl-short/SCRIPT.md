# The Cleaning Crew: why your stomach growls (Bean Stories short #16) - target <= 40 s

## Facts (sourced)
- Stomach growling = borborygmi: gas + fluid pushed by gut contractions (peristalsis); from the stomach and small
  intestine. It happens during digestion too, but food dampens the sound; an empty gut is louder.
- Migrating motor complex (MMC): in the fasting state, a cyclic "housekeeper" pattern (~90-120 min cycle); phase III =
  strong contraction waves sweeping from the stomach through the small intestine, clearing leftover food, mucus and
  bacteria (Szurszewski 1969; Deloose et al. 2012, Nat Rev Gastroenterol Hepatol).
- Ghrelin (the hunger hormone) rises before habitual meal times (Cummings et al. 2001, Diabetes) and can trigger
  premature phase III activity (Tack et al. 2006, Gut).

## Voice-over (Liam, speed 1.1)
Your stomach growling doesn't mean it's hungry. It means it's cleaning. When your stomach goes empty, a cleaning crew wakes up. Every hour or two, a giant squeeze sweeps down your gut, pushing out every last crumb. But with no food in there, it's just air and juice. Squish it, and it echoes. That's the growl. Your stomach does this when it's full too. You just can't hear it. Food muffles the sound. Now the weird part. The crew runs on a schedule, so it starts right before you usually eat. Which is why it always growls... in the quietest room possible.
