# Wrong Address: why ice cream gives you brain freeze (Bean Stories short #15) - target <= 40 s

## Facts (sourced)
- Brain freeze = "ice-cream headache" / sphenopalatine ganglioneuralgia (ICHD-3 7.x "headache attributed to ingestion
  or inhalation of a cold stimulus"). Brain tissue itself has no pain receptors; pain comes from vessels/meninges/nerves.
- Cold on the palate -> rapid constriction then dilation of local blood vessels (and a surge of flow in the anterior
  cerebral artery: Serrador et al. 2012, FASEB J. abstract). Signal travels via the trigeminal nerve, which also
  supplies the forehead -> referred pain felt in the forehead/temples.
- Kaczorowski M & Kaczorowski J. "Ice cream evoked headaches (ICE-H) study: randomised trial of accelerated versus
  cautious ice cream eating regimen." BMJ 2002;325:1445. Daughter (a schoolkid) + father; 145 students; fast eaters
  ~27% vs slow ~13% brain freeze (about double).
- Common fix: warm the palate (tongue / warm drink); slow down.

## Voice-over (Liam, speed 1.1)
Brain freeze isn't happening in your brain. Your brain can't even feel pain. The cold hits the roof of your mouth. The blood vessels there squeeze shut... then blast wide open. A nerve rings the alarm. But that same nerve also wires up your forehead. So your brain gets confused... and blames your face. Now the weird part. A schoolkid and her dad tested this. Half the kids ate ice cream fast. Half ate it slow. The fast ones? Twice the brain freeze. The fix? Press your warm tongue to the roof of your mouth. Or... eat slower. Nah.
