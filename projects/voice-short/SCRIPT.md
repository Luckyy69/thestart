# Your Voice on Recordings (Bean Stories short #11) - target <= 40 s

## Facts (sourced)
- You hear your own voice via air conduction AND bone conduction; the skull conducts lower frequencies better, so you
  perceive your voice lower/fuller; a recording (air only) sounds higher/thinner to you - but that is how others hear
  you. (Scientific American "Why does my voice sound so different when it is recorded"; Popular Science; Live Science;
  Wikipedia "Bone conduction")
- Hughes & Harrison 2013, Perception 42(9):941-949 "I like my voice better: self-enhancement bias in perceptions of
  voice attractiveness": 80 participants rated recordings (counting 1-10) unaware their own was among them; they rated
  their own voice more attractive than others rated it and more than they rated other voices ("vocal implicit egotism").
  (Penn State/Albright College; ScienceDaily 2013)

## Voice-over (Liam)
That voice you hear on recordings? Bad news. That's the real you.
When you talk, you hear yourself twice.
Once through the air, like everyone else.
And once through your own skull. Your bones carry the sound straight to your ears, and make it deeper.
A microphone only catches the air part.
So that weird voice is the one everyone has heard... your whole life.
But here's the twist.
In one study, people rated a bunch of recorded voices, without knowing theirs was in there.
Their own voice? They rated it higher than everyone else's.
You don't hate your voice. You just don't recognize it.
