# Bald Fur: why we get goosebumps (Bean Stories short #14) - target <= 40 s

## Facts (sourced)
- Goosebumps (piloerection / cutis anserina): each hair follicle has a tiny smooth muscle, the arrector pili. The
  sympathetic nervous system contracts them on cold or fear; the hair stands up and the skin around it bulges.
- In furry mammals the same reflex fluffs the fur: traps an insulating air layer and makes the animal look bigger to
  threats (cats, porcupines, chimpanzees). In humans, with little body hair, it is a vestigial reflex.
  (Darwin 1872, The Expression of the Emotions; Chaplin et al. 2014 review; Shwartz et al. 2020, Cell - arrector pili
  muscle + sympathetic nerve also act on hair follicle stem cells.)
- Music chills / "frisson": strong emotional peaks in music trigger piloerection along with other sympathetic arousal
  signs (Blood & Zatorre 2001, PNAS; Benedek & Kaernbach 2011, Biol Psychol; Sachs et al. 2016).

## Voice-over (Liam, speed 1.1)
Goosebumps are your body trying to puff up fur... you don't even have. Every tiny hair on your skin has its own tiny muscle. When you get cold or scared, your nerves pull them all at once. Hairs stand up. Skin bumps up. On a cat, this works. The fur puffs up, traps warm air, and makes it look twice as big. On you... bumps. Your hairy ancestors had the full coat. You kept the button... but lost the coat. Now the weird part. Music can press that same button. That shiver when the song hits? Your body is puffing up to look scary... for a song. Very scary. Very bald.
