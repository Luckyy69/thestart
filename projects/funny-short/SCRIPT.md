# Live Wire: why hitting your funny bone zaps your pinky (Bean Stories short #17) - target <= 40 s

## Facts (sourced)
- The "funny bone" is not a bone: it is the ulnar nerve where it passes behind the medial epicondyle of the
  humerus (cubital tunnel), covered only by skin and a little fat (standard anatomy; e.g. Cleveland Clinic,
  "Funny Bone"; Gray's Anatomy, ulnar nerve course C8-T1 from the brachial plexus to the hand).
- Ulnar nerve supplies sensation to the little finger and half the ring finger -> a knock at the elbow is felt
  as tingling/zapping in those fingers (referred along the nerve's territory; projected sensation).
- Name: commonly explained as a pun on "humerus" / "humorous"; the term "funny bone" is attested in English since
  at least the 1840s (Oxford English Dictionary; Barham's Ingoldsby Legends, 1840). Origin of the pun is uncertain
  -> say "probably".

## Voice-over (Liam, speed 1.1)
Your funny bone isn't a bone. And it's definitely not funny. It's a nerve. It runs from your neck all the way down to your pinky. And at your elbow, it squeezes through a tiny gap with no padding. Just skin. So when you whack it, you're basically hitting a live wire. The whole line lights up... and your pinky screams, even though your pinky is totally fine. Now the weird part. Why "funny"? Probably a pun. The bone right there is called the humerus. Humerus... humorous. People have been making that joke for almost two hundred years. It's still not funny.
