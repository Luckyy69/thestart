A passenger jet ran out of fuel at forty-one thousand feet, because someone used the wrong units. It's 1983, and Canada is switching to the metric system. Air Canada's brand new Boeing 767 measures its fuel in kilograms. But on the ground, the fuel is worked out in pounds. It's like a recipe that says two cups of flour, and you add two spoons. The number looks right, but there's far too little. So the plane takes off with less than half the fuel it needs. High over Canada, a warning beeps. Then the left engine stops. Then the right one. Most of the screens in the cockpit go dark. Luckily, the captain flies gliders as a hobby. Unluckily, the only runway they can reach has become a racetrack. And tonight, it's full of families, campers and go-karts. So the captain tips the jet sideways, a glider trick nobody had ever tried in an airliner, and it drops like a stone without speeding up. The front wheel collapses, and the nose scrapes down the runway in a shower of sparks. The racers run over with fire extinguishers. All sixty-nine people on board walk away. And the plane? It was repaired, and flew for another twenty-five years, as the Gimli Glider.
