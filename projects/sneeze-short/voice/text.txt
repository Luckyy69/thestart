Up to one in four people sneeze when they look at the sun. Maybe you're one of them. Over two thousand years ago, Aristotle had a theory. The sun heats your nose, your nose sweats... and you sneeze. Two thousand years later, Francis Bacon tested it. He faced the sun with his eyes closed. The heat was there. The sneeze wasn't. So it's not your nose. It's your eyes. Today, scientists think it's crossed wires. The nerve that makes you sneeze runs right next to the nerve that carries light from your eyes. Bright light floods in... and your brain mixes up the signals. Light? Must be something in the nose! Achoo! And it runs in families. If one parent has it, each kid has about a fifty percent chance. But here's the best part. Scientists gave it an official name. Autosomal Dominant Compelling Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst. Or for short... ACHOO.
