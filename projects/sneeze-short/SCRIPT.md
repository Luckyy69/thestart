# The Sun Sneeze (Bean Stories short #9)

## Facts (sourced)
- Photic sneeze reflex: estimates range roughly 10-35% of people; a Chinese study of 3,417 people found 25.6%
  (PubMed 27568353 / PMC); Scientific American "Looking at the Sun Can Trigger a Sneeze" (informal surveys 10-35%).
- Aristotle (~350 BCE, Problems): "Why does the heat of the sun provoke sneezing?" - thought the sun's heat made the nose
  sweat. Francis Bacon (1635, Sylva Sylvarum) faced the sun with his eyes closed: heat, but no sneeze.
  (Scientific American, Cosmos, Gizmodo, Wikipedia "Photic sneeze reflex")
- Modern view: "crossed wires" - the trigeminal nerve (sneeze) lies close to the optic nerve; bright light signals are
  misread as nasal irritation (Everett 1964 "optic-trigeminal summation"). Inherited, autosomal dominant: if one parent
  has it, about half the children will.
- 1978 paper proposed the name "Autosomal Dominant Compelling Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst syndrome" = ACHOO.

## Voice-over (Liam)
Up to one in four people sneeze when they look at the sun. Maybe you're one of them.
Over two thousand years ago, Aristotle had a theory. The sun heats your nose, your nose sweats... and you sneeze.
Two thousand years later, Francis Bacon tested it. He faced the sun with his eyes closed.
The heat was there. The sneeze wasn't.
So it's not your nose. It's your eyes.
Today, scientists think it's crossed wires.
The nerve that makes you sneeze runs right next to the nerve that carries light from your eyes.
Bright light floods in... and your brain mixes up the signals.
Light? Must be something in the nose!
Achoo!
And it runs in families. If one parent has it, each kid has about a fifty percent chance.
But here's the best part. Scientists gave it an official name.
Autosomal Dominant Compelling Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst.
Or for short... ACHOO.
