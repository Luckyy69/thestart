# Rain Tires: why fingers wrinkle in the bath (Bean Stories short #13) - target <= 40 s

## Facts (sourced)
- Water-induced finger wrinkling is an active process controlled by the sympathetic nervous system: the blood vessels
  under the skin constrict (vasoconstriction), the fingertip pulp loses volume and the skin folds. Fingers with a cut
  (denervated) median nerve do not wrinkle (Lewis & Pickering 1935; used clinically as a nerve-function test;
  Wilder-Smith & Chow 2003, "Water-immersion wrinkling is due to vasoconstriction").
- "Rain treads" hypothesis: the wrinkle pattern channels water away like tire treads (Changizi et al. 2011, Brain
  Behav Evol).
- Experiment: wrinkled fingers moved wet marbles/objects faster than unwrinkled ones (Kareklas, Nettle & Smulders
  2013, Biology Letters). (A 2014 study did not replicate this, so the purpose is framed as "scientists think".)

## Voice-over (Liam, speed 1.1, 36.9 s)
Your wrinkly bath fingers? That's not water soaking in. It's your brain... putting on rain tires.
Here's the weird part. If the nerve in a finger is cut, that finger never wrinkles.
So your body is doing this on purpose.
When your hands stay wet, your nerves squeeze the blood vessels under the skin.
The fingertips shrink... and fold into grooves. Just like the tread on a tire.
In one experiment, people moved wet marbles faster with wrinkly fingers.
Scientists think it helped our ancestors grip wet rocks and branches in the rain.
So next time you're stuck in the bath... your hands aren't ruined. They just switched to rain mode.
