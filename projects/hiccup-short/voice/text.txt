Your hiccups might be left over from when your ancestors lived in water. A hiccup is your diaphragm suddenly spasming. You gasp... and your throat slams shut. Hic. But why would a body ever do that? Some scientists think it's an ancient move from tadpoles. They gulp... and snap their throat shut, so water flows over their gills, not into their lungs. Millions of years later, you're still doing it. Usually after a fizzy drink. And sometimes... it doesn't stop. In 1922, a farmer named Charles Osborne started hiccuping. He kept going for sixty-eight years. About four hundred thirty million hics.
