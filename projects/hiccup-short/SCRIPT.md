# Hiccups Come From Tadpoles (Bean Stories short #10) - target <= 40 s

## Facts (sourced)
- A hiccup = a sudden involuntary contraction of the diaphragm (inspiration) followed ~35 ms later by glottal closure
  -> the "hic". (Straus et al., BioEssays 2003; Dartmouth DUJS "Why Do We Hiccup?")
- Hypothesis (Straus et al. 2003, "A phylogenetic hypothesis for the origin of hiccough"): hiccups are a leftover of the
  gill-ventilation motor pattern of amphibian tadpoles, which breathe water and air: inspiration with glottal closure
  so water passes over the gills and does not enter the lungs. Shared traits: glottal closure during inspiration,
  inhibited by CO2, abolished by baclofen. It is a hypothesis; other theories exist (Howes: venting air in sucklings).
- Charles Osborne (Iowa farmer) hiccuped from 1922 (while weighing a hog) to 1990: 68 years, an estimated 430 million
  hiccups. Guinness World Records "Longest attack of hiccups"; Smithsonian; Wikipedia.

## Voice-over (Liam)
Your hiccups might be left over from when your ancestors lived in water.
A hiccup is your diaphragm suddenly spasming. You gasp... and your throat slams shut. Hic.
But why would a body ever do that?
Some scientists think it's an ancient move from tadpoles.
They gulp... and snap their throat shut, so water flows over their gills, not into their lungs.
Millions of years later, you're still doing it. Usually after a fizzy drink.
And sometimes... it doesn't stop.
In 1922, a farmer named Charles Osborne started hiccuping. He kept going for sixty-eight years.
About four hundred thirty million hics.
